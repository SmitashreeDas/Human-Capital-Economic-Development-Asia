1. Introduction

Economic development is influenced by several social and labour-market factors in addition to income.

This project examines the relationship between GDP per capita and selected human capital indicators across ten Asian countries from 2011 to 2024.

The analysis uses three indicators:

  • GDP per capita
  • Life expectancy at birth
  • Youth labour force participation rate (ages 15–24)

The study focuses on country-wise comparisons, trends and relationships between these indicators.

Research Question

Does higher human capital and youth labour force participation correspond with higher GDP per capita across selected Asian countries?

Countries Covered

The analysis includes:

  • India
  • Bangladesh
  • Nepal
  • Sri Lanka
  • China
  • Japan
  • Korea, Rep.
  • Indonesia
  • Malaysia
  • Thailand

2. Data Source

The data are obtained from the World Development Indicators (WDI) database of the World Bank.

The analysis covers the period 2011–2024.

The variables used are:

  1. GDP per capita (constant 2015 US$)
  2. Life expectancy at birth, total (years)
  3. Labour force participation rate for ages 15–24, total (%), modeled ILO estimate

#Findings GDP per capita generally increased across the selected Asian countries from 2011 to 2024.

Japan had the highest average GDP per capita and life expectancy among the selected countries.

Youth labour force participation showed considerable differences between countries.

GDP per capita had a strong positive correlation with life expectancy (0.89).

GDP per capita had almost no correlation with youth labour force participation (-0.01).

The regression results show that life expectancy had a significant positive relationship with GDP per capita, while youth labour force participation was not statistically significant.

#Conclusion This study examined GDP per capita, life expectancy and youth labour force participation across ten selected Asian countries from 2011 to 2024. The results show a strong positive relationship between GDP per capita and life expectancy. However, youth labour force participation did not show a clear relationship with GDP per capita. Overall, the analysis highlights differences in economic and social indicators across the selected Asian countries.
